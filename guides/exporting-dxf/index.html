Start with the flat cutting shape

Your DXF needs the outside edge of the part and every intended internal cut-out. Remove assembly views, dimensions, notes, title blocks and spare geometry. Keep a copy of your editable original design.

For a sheet-metal part, use the appropriate flat pattern rather than a perspective or folded projection. Suitable STEP/STP models can also go straight to quoting; review the prepared flat drawing and any unfolding warnings. We do not offer bending; parts are supplied flat and deburred.

Export at 1:1 in millimetres

Set the drawing units to millimetres and the export scale to full size. Drawing-sheet or print scale can change the exported size if you export a page instead of the part geometry.

Measure after export

Reopen the exported DXF and measure the overall size plus a known hole or feature. A preview that looks correct can still be the wrong size.

Keep only clean cut paths

  • Use closed outlines for the outside profile and intended cut-outs.
  • Remove duplicate or stacked lines.
  • Join endpoints where a profile should be continuous.
  • Trim unintended crossings and remove hidden drawing aids.
  • Convert text that should be cut to actual curves or outlines.
  • Add permanent bridges to lettering centres you want to keep.

The visual cutting guide shows these checks with examples.

Choose the geometry export in your software

CAD software may offer DXF export from a sketch, face, drawing or sheet-metal flat pattern. Choose the source that represents the actual flat part. Avoid exporting a drawing sheet with dimensions and title blocks.

In vector software, convert intended cut text to paths and check closed shapes before export. If your tool exports SVG instead of a suitable DXF, use File Workshop and review the prepared geometry and finished size.

Menu names and export options vary by product and version. Use your software’s guidance for the selected geometry, then apply the same dimension and path checks to the resulting file.

Review the quote preview

  1. Upload the DXF and check the selected part.
  2. Confirm the overall width and height.
  3. Check the position and size of every hole and slot.
  4. Select the intended material, thickness and quantity.
  5. Read warnings, and review any proposed repair before continuing.

If the result differs from your drawing, stop and correct it before paying. Contact us if you are unsure which geometry should be cut.

Starting with another file?

File Workshop accepts suitable SVG, vector PDF, JPG, PNG and DWG inputs as well as drawing repairs. Image inputs need a sharp black silhouette on white, followed by a real finished dimension; it cannot recover exact dimensions from a perspective photograph.
