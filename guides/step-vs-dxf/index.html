DXF or STEP?
Which file should you upload?

DXF is a flat 2D drawing of the shape to cut. STEP is a 3D model. We can use suitable sheet-metal STEP models too, but all parts are cut and supplied flat. Here’s how to choose your file.

.step and .stp are two file extensions for the same format. Either can be used for a suitable sheet-metal model.

Use DXF for a flat outline

A DXF tells us where to cut: the outside edge of your part and each hole or slot. For a plate, panel or spacer, export a flat view of those edges. Leave out dimensions, notes and other views.

  • Export at full size (1:1) in millimetres.
  • Include only the lines you want cut.
  • Check for gaps in outlines and duplicate lines.
  • Reopen the exported DXF and check its measurements.

Our DXF export guide explains what to include and what to leave out.

Use STEP for a sheet-metal model

The model must represent one sheet-metal part with a consistent thickness. A folded model needs to unfold into a flat cutting shape. Check that flat shape in the preview, including the holes, slots and any unfolding warnings.

We supply the part flat, without bending

A folded model helps describe the design, but we supply the flat part cut from sheet. Arrange any bending separately. If the finished bend positions or fit matter, confirm the flat dimensions and bend allowances with whoever will bend the part.

File Workshop edits the flat drawing prepared from your model. If the model does not unfold correctly, fix it in your design software or export a correct flat DXF. See our sheet-metal example.

Check the preview before you order

Whichever file you use, compare the preview with your design. Check the outside shape, width, height and every hole and slot. Then select your material, thickness and quantity in the quote, even if the model already has a thickness.

Metal parts include deburring. Acrylic is CO₂ laser cut and does not need deburring. If a part must fit precisely or needs a particular surface finish, read our quality guide and discuss the details with us before ordering. Our ordering guide explains the rest of the process.
