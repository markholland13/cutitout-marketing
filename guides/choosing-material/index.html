Start with the requirements

  • Will the part be exposed to moisture, chemicals, heat or wear?
  • Does weight or rigidity matter?
  • Will a face remain visible, or will it be painted or coated?
  • Will it be bent, welded, tapped, bonded or assembled later?
  • Does the drawing specify a grade or a required certificate?

A material comparison is a starting point. Use the grade and properties required for your application, and check availability before ordering.

Materials behave differently even when the outline and thickness match. Changing the material may require changes to the design.

Choose thickness with the features in mind

Thickness affects weight and rigidity, and changes the available cutting limits. A small hole that works in one sheet may be outside the online range for a thicker sheet. Check both the material range and the minimum hole and slot sizes.

Specify the thickness needed for the finished job. If strength or structural performance is essential, establish that requirement in your design before placing the cutting order.

Decide what the finished surface should do

Raw metal faces, cut edges and coated surfaces have different appearances. Confirm any decorative finish, visible face or colour requirement before ordering. Textured powder coating is available on supported metal jobs.

For acrylic, check the chosen colour and clarity. If you need an exact match to an existing part, a named sheet specification is more useful than a screen image.
