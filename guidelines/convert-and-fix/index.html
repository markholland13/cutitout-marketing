Which file have you got?

Ready DXF, STEP and STP files can go straight to quoting. Use File Workshop for other formats or to review drawing repairs.

2D CUTTING DRAWING

DXF / DWG

A flat drawing at full size in millimetres. Include only the lines to cut; remove dimensions and drawing borders.

VECTOR ARTWORK

SVG / PDF

Use vector outlines: shapes made from lines and curves, rather than pixels. Convert lettering to outlines. A PDF must be one page and contain no photos or other embedded images.

BLACK & WHITE ARTWORK

JPG / PNG

A sharp, solid black shape on white. Check the traced detail and set the finished size yourself.

See good and bad examples ↓
SHEET-METAL MODEL

STEP / STP

A model of one sheet-metal part with a consistent thickness. Suitable models are unfolded into a flat drawing before editing. We supply the part flat, without bending. Metal parts include deburring; acrylic does not need it.

See the sheet-metal example ↓

DWG needs the conversion service to be available. AI, BMP, TIFF and STL are not accepted by File Workshop. Export suitable DXF or SVG paths from your original software instead.

02 / JPG & PNG: KEEP IT BLACK AND WHITE

A silhouette.
Not a photograph.

Export flat, front-on artwork from your software.
No shading, shadows or perspective.

Black stays.
White is cut away.

File Workshop traces the boundaries between black and white. Every unwanted mark can become another outline.

Black = material to keepWhite inside = holes & cutouts

Show the whole part with a little white space around it. Make small holes and gaps clear enough to see.

For JPG and PNG, use a filled silhouette. A thin line drawing can trace both sides of each line. Export vector paths as DXF or SVG when you have them.

BEFORE EXPORTING FROM YOUR SOFTWARE

Export the part.
Leave out notes and borders.

  • Export just the part. Remove backgrounds, borders, dimensions, logos and notes you do not want cut.
  • For images: black on white. Use a filled, front-on shape with crisp edges. Check that every hole remains visible.
  • For vector files: use lines and curves. Close outlines, remove duplicates, and convert text intended for cutting to curves or outlines.
  • Check scale after export. Use 1:1 in millimetres for CAD. Images need a real finished dimension set in File Workshop.

PDF: WHAT’S INSIDE MATTERS

PDF file?
Check it contains vector outlines.

Export one page of lines and curves from your design software. A photo or scan saved as a PDF is not suitable.

Multi-page PDFs and PDFs containing embedded images are rejected, even if some vector lines are present.

SAME IMAGE. DIFFERENT FINISHED SIZE.

Set the size you want made.

Enter a known width or height. Keep proportions locked so circles stay round. Unlocking proportions stretches the part and changes its features.

03 / ALREADY HAVE A DXF?

Check the problem.
Compare before and after.

File Workshop can help with gaps, duplicate lines and other drawing problems. Check each proposed repair against your original design.

Gaps in outlinesDuplicate linesUnwanted linesCurves made of short linesLoose letter centres

Close a small gap.

The editor can join some small gaps between line ends. Check that the new line keeps the correct shape. For a large gap or missing edge, fix the original drawing. Leave any intentional slit open.

Cut each edge once.

Extra copies of the same line can be removed while keeping one copy of the edge. Check the result before accepting it. If separate parts overlap, correct their positions in your original drawing.

Replay shows the same outside edge twice on the left and once on the right.

Remove unwanted lines.

Remove dimensions, borders, construction lines and unwanted marks. If there are several parts or views, select the part you want cut. Keep all its holes and slots.

Check circles made of short lines.

Some circles and curves are exported as lots of short straight lines. File Workshop may suggest replacing them with a circle or smooth curve. Compare Original and Prepared before accepting it, and keep any straight sections that belong in the design.

Keep letter centres attached.

Why bridges matter ↗

Bridges are strips of material left uncut to hold a centre in place, such as the middle of an ‘O’. Check their width and position before accepting them. Leave holes open where you want the middle to fall out. Blue shows material left uncut; the sizes shown are examples.

SOME ISSUES NEED YOUR DECISION

Check that the repair keeps your design.

  • Missing lines or unclear artwork: fix the original design or use a clearer image.
  • Holes, slots and part size: check the cutting limits for your material and thickness.
  • A STEP model that will not unfold: correct the model in your design software. File Workshop edits its flat drawing.
