01 / TEXT & BRIDGES

Keep the centres in place.

The middle of a letter is a separate piece.
A small bridge keeps it connected.

The same rule applies to enclosed shapes in logos.
A, O, P, R… and logos too ＋

Any enclosed shape needs a connection if you want its centre to stay in the finished part. Add bridges in your drawing and make their width suitable for your material. If the centre should fall out, leave it unconnected. These permanent design bridges are different from temporary manufacturing micro-joints.

Minimum size or functional fit? ＋

The minimum is the smallest internal feature listed for cutting. A hole for a bolt or pin also needs clearance for the intended fit. Narrow connections left between cuts need their own care; the minimum hole size is not a bridge-width rule.

Which fit should I choose? ＋

Choose the closer clearance when you want less movement around a pass-through bolt. Choose the larger clearance for easier assembly. For bearings, locating pins, tapped holes or a critical fit, ask us before ordering.

A little more help.

What are micro-joints? ＋

Micro-joints are temporary tabs used during manufacturing to hold a part securely in the sheet. A small mark can remain after removal. They are different from the permanent stencil bridges you draw to retain the centres of letters.

What if my quote flags a problem? ＋

Review the highlighted feature, check the chosen material and thickness, then adjust your drawing and upload the revised DXF. If you are unsure, ask us to take a look.

My part needs a precise fit. Can you help? ＋

For bearings, locating pins, threaded holes or other critical fits, contact us before ordering. The clearance examples above are for bolts passing through the part.
